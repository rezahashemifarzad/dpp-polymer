## 2.2 Revenue streams

The platform is designed around recurring, usage-aligned B2B SaaS revenue rather than one-off project fees, reflecting that a Digital Product Passport only has value if it stays live and current for the lifetime of every batch it describes.

1. **Tiered manufacturer subscription (core revenue line).** Monthly/annual SaaS fee per manufacturer account, tiered by batch volume (number of passports issued/managed per month) and feature access (e.g. base tier: passport creation, QR/GS1 generation, public passport page; higher tiers: batch genealogy/traceability, carbon footprint calculator, ESG/PPWR analytics dashboards, green-tax estimation, supplier performance ranking, bulk certificate/label generation).
2. **Auditor/verification seat licensing.** A per-seat or per-verification fee for the independent Auditor Portal, reflecting that auditors are a distinct, professionally liable user class whose access must be individually credentialed and priced separately from the manufacturer's own internal users.
3. **B2B buyer/API access.** A stable, versioned Buyer API (`/api/v1/client/*`) for OEMs and converters who want verified supplier passport data flowing directly into their own ERP/CSRD reporting systems, priced per connected buyer account or per data-volume tier — this is the layer that turns the platform from a single-manufacturer tool into supply-chain infrastructure.
4. **Compliance export and certificate services.** Usage-based or bundled fees for generated artifacts with real commercial value on their own — PDF Certificates of Conformity, printable QR label sheets for factory-line batch labeling, and audit-ready compliance export workbooks (Excel exports with a live hash-chain integrity check embedded) that a manufacturer's own auditors or regulators will request directly.
5. **Onboarding, data migration, and integration services.** A one-time professional-services fee for larger accounts migrating historical batch/certification data from spreadsheets or legacy systems, and for bespoke ERP integrations — priced separately from the recurring SaaS fee so it does not distort the platform's core unit economics.
6. **(Future/optional) White-label / multi-tenant licensing.** Once the platform's multi-role architecture (manufacturer, auditor, buyer, supplier-invite) is proven at scale, a white-label deployment for an industry association, a certification body, or a national/regional chemical cluster becomes a plausible higher-value, lower-volume revenue line — flagged here as a post-MVP expansion option, not a Year 1 assumption.

## 2.3 Cost structure

- **Engineering and product development** — the dominant cost line in the current MVP/early-growth phase: full-stack engineering (Next.js/TypeScript/Prisma), ongoing regulatory-schema maintenance as ESPR delegated acts and PPWR guidance evolve, and UX/design work to keep the B2B dashboard and consumer QR experience at an enterprise-credible standard.
- **Cloud infrastructure** — managed PostgreSQL (Neon), hosting/compute (Vercel-class platform), and the marginal cost of PDF/QR generation at scale; structurally low per-passport cost since the architecture is built to be stateless and compute-light per request.
- **Compliance and domain expertise** — ongoing input from polymer-science and EU-regulatory expertise (the Domain-Expert and Compliance-Legal functions in this project's own working model) to keep material-data schemas and compliance mappings correct as standards evolve; this is a genuine, recurring cost, not a one-time setup task, because ESPR/PPWR delegated acts will continue to be published over the coming years.
- **Sales, partnerships, and customer success** — direct B2B sales effort into the NRW/DACH polymer manufacturing base, auditor network development and training, and account management for existing manufacturer customers (churn in this business is existential — a customer's entire batch history and public QR resolvers depend on continuity of service).
- **Security, audit, and certification maintenance** — periodic third-party security review of the cryptographic audit ledger and RBAC boundaries, and maintaining current alignment with ISCC PLUS/REDcert2/ISO 14021/GS1 Digital Link scheme requirements as those standards bodies update their own rules.
- **Regulatory/grant compliance overhead** — reporting and documentation obligations that come with public co-financing (NRW.BANK/EFRE.NRW programs typically require project reporting, state-aid/de-minimis documentation, and impact reporting) — a real but bounded cost that this grant package itself is part of managing.

## 2.4 Target market: NRW and EU

**Immediate target market — NRW's polymer and chemical processing base.** NRW is one of the largest chemical-industry regions in Europe by employment and output, anchored by the Rhineland chemical corridor (Cologne–Leverkusen–Dormagen–Krefeld) and a dense Ruhr-area recycling and plastics-processing sector. The primary buyer persona is a compounder, mechanical or chemical recycler, or resin converter in the 50–500 employee range that already produces PCR/PIR or bio-based grades (or is under commercial pressure from OEM customers to start) and does not have in-house software capacity to build ESPR/PPWR-compliant traceability itself.

**Adjacent target — EU-wide expansion via the same regulatory driver.** Because ESPR and PPWR are EU regulations, not German national law, every polymer manufacturer across the EU faces the same compliance deadline. NRW is the beachhead market (home region, RWTH Aachen ecosystem access, dense customer concentration for efficient early sales), with a clear expansion path into the wider DACH chemical cluster (Rhine-Main, Bavaria, Austria, German-speaking Switzerland) and then the Benelux/broader EU polymer manufacturing base once the NRW reference customer base and case studies are established.

**Why NRW specifically as the beachhead, not just "Germany":** proximity to the RWTH Aachen Center for Circular Economy and Fraunhofer institutes provides a credible technical-validation partner for the platform's material-science claims (see Section 3), and NRW's own strategic priorities — sustainability, digitalization of traditional industry, and circular economy — are explicit funding and policy priorities of the state government and NRW.BANK, which is the structural basis for the grant alignment argued in Section 3.
