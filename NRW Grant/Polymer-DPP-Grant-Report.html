01 — Executive Summary & Value Proposition

A verifiable trust asset, not another compliance spreadsheet

💡
Key takeaway

A working, deployed DPP platform — not a concept — that turns the EU's incoming ESPR/PPWR transparency mandate into a verifiable trust asset for NRW's polymer and chemical Mittelstand, built on cryptographic tamper-evidence and GS1-standard QR verification.

The problem

Europe's polymer value chain is being forced, in a short window, to prove things it has historically never had to prove: exactly how much recycled or bio-based content is in a given batch of resin, where that feedstock came from, what its carbon footprint is, and whether every claim on the label is independently verifiable rather than self-declared. The EU's Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR), the Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR, (EU) 2025/40), and the broader Digital Product Passport mandate turn "trust us" sustainability marketing into a hard compliance requirement with legal exposure attached — the EU's Green Claims direction explicitly targets unsubstantiated environmental claims.

For polymer compounders, recyclers, and converters — an industry segment heavily concentrated in NRW's Rhein-Ruhr and Rhineland chemical corridor — this creates an operational problem that spreadsheets and PDFs cannot solve at scale: mass-balance chain-of-custody (ISCC PLUS, REDcert2) has to be tracked batch-by-batch, certifications have to expire and get re-verified on a schedule, carbon footprint figures have to be distinguishable from marketing estimates, and every one of these facts has to be exposed through a QR-code-scannable, GS1-Digital-Link-compliant passport by the time ESPR's delegated acts start applying to plastics value chains.

Most mid-sized polymer processors — the Mittelstand backbone of NRW's chemical and plastics cluster — have neither the software engineering capacity nor the regulatory staff to build this themselves, and the generic ERP/PLM vendors serving this space were not built around DPP-specific data models.

What we have built

Polymer DPP is a working, deployed SaaS platform that gives polymer manufacturers, recyclers, and compounders a purpose-built Digital Product Passport system:

  • Industrial-grade material data capture — ISO 1133 Melt Flow Index, density, tensile strength, PCR/PIR recycled content, bio-based content, and PEF/LCA-based carbon footprint, stored with exact decimal precision.
  • Multi-party trust workflow — manufacturers submit batch data; independent, individually-credentialed third-party auditors formally verify or reject it before it is ever shown to a downstream buyer or consumer as fact.
  • Cryptographic immutability — every batch record is SHA-256 sealed, and every state change is written to an append-only, hash-chained audit ledger with a live integrity-verification function.
  • Consumer- and auditor-facing transparency — a GS1 Digital Link QR code on every batch resolves to a public passport page, plus dedicated auditor and buyer portals and a full ESG/compliance analytics suite.
  • Regulatory-adjacent analytics — a computed Sustainability Readiness Index, PPWR 2030/2040 target tracking, supplier performance ranking, and illustrative EU plastic-tax exposure.

The value proposition

For manufacturers and recyclers: turn an unavoidable compliance burden into a competitive credential — a QR code any auditor can scan beats "we say our resin is 40% PCR."

For B2B buyers: a single point of truth for the sustainability data they must themselves report under their own CSRD/ESPR obligations, without re-keying supplier claims.

For NRW as a region: a homegrown, EU-compliant DPP infrastructure layer for one of Germany's largest chemical and plastics industrial clusters, reducing dependence on non-EU vendors for a function that becomes mandatory across the entire European polymer value chain within this decade.

Why now, why NRW

NRW is home to one of Europe's densest concentrations of chemical, plastics-processing, and recycling capacity, sitting directly alongside a world-class materials-science research base at RWTH Aachen. ESPR's delegated acts and PPWR's 2030 recycled-content thresholds create a hard deadline measured in a small number of years — the window to become NRW's default DPP infrastructure provider is open now.

Revenue streams — the detail

The platform is designed around recurring, usage-aligned B2B SaaS revenue rather than one-off project fees, reflecting that a Digital Product Passport only has value if it stays live and current for the lifetime of every batch it describes.

  • Tiered manufacturer subscription — the core revenue line, tiered by batch volume and feature access (base: passport creation, QR/GS1, public page; higher: genealogy, carbon calculator, ESG/PPWR analytics, green-tax estimation, bulk certificate/label generation).
  • Auditor/verification seat licensing — auditors are a distinct, professionally liable user class, individually credentialed and priced separately.
  • B2B buyer/API access — a stable, versioned Buyer API for OEMs and converters feeding verified supplier data into their own ERP/CSRD reporting, priced per connected account or data volume.
  • Compliance export & certificate services — usage-based fees for PDF Certificates of Conformity, printable QR label sheets, and audit-ready compliance export workbooks with an embedded live hash-chain integrity check.
  • Onboarding, migration & integration services — one-time professional-services fees for larger accounts, priced separately from recurring SaaS revenue.
  • (Future) White-label / multi-tenant licensing — a plausible higher-value, lower-volume line once the multi-role architecture is proven at scale — flagged as a post-MVP option, not a Year 1 assumption.

Cost structure — the detail

Engineering and product development dominate the early-stage cost line, alongside cloud infrastructure (structurally low per-passport cost thanks to a stateless, compute-light architecture), ongoing compliance and domain expertise (a recurring cost, not a one-time setup, as ESPR/PPWR delegated acts continue to be published), sales and customer success (churn is existential here — a customer's entire batch history and public QR resolvers depend on continuity of service), periodic security/audit review, and the reporting overhead that comes with public co-financing.

Target market: NRW and EU

Immediate: NRW's polymer and chemical processing base — the Rhineland chemical corridor and Ruhr-area recycling sector — targeting compounders, recyclers, and converters (50–500 employees) already producing PCR/PIR or bio-based grades. Adjacent: because ESPR and PPWR are EU regulations, every polymer manufacturer across the EU faces the same deadline — NRW is the beachhead (home region, RWTH Aachen access, dense customer concentration), with a clear expansion path into DACH and then Benelux/broader EU.

04 — Technical Architecture Summary

What's actually live — written for a grant reviewer

💡
Key takeaway

Every capability below is live in the production codebase today — a cryptographic hash-chained audit ledger, GS1 Digital Link QR, a multi-role RBAC trust workflow, and PPWR-aware ESG analytics — not a roadmap promise.

Next.js (App Router) TypeScript PostgreSQL — Neon Prisma ORM Prisma.Decimal precision SHA-256 hash-chained ledger GS1 Digital Link @react-pdf/renderer

A grant reviewer evaluating a digitalization or circular-economy project typically needs to answer two questions beyond "does the idea make sense": is there a real, working system behind this application, and is the technical approach sound enough to scale and survive regulatory/security scrutiny. This section answers both directly — the platform is not a prototype, it is a deployed application with a live PostgreSQL database, real API routes, and multiple working user-facing portals.

The Digital Product Passport data model

At the center of the schema is PolymerPassport — one row per physical batch — holding GTIN, batch ID, polymer type and grade, ISO 1133 melt flow index, density, tensile strength, recycled content (PCR/PIR distinction), bio-based content, and a verified carbon footprint figure, optionally third-party-verified. Every passport carries a SHA-256 recordHash and, once revised, a previousRecordHash, so the record's own history is independently checkable.

Built on top of this core, already live: batch genealogy (a recursive upstream traceability tree, capped at a bounded depth for query safety); a carbon footprint calculator kept strictly separate from the manufacturer's own verified LCA figure and always labeled illustrative; a certification workflow (ISCC PLUS, ISO 14021, EN 15343, REDcert2) with a formal pending-review → verified/rejected lifecycle, with expiry computed at read time rather than stored as a manually maintained flag; and a real GS1 Digital Link QR resolver with a printable label-sheet suite for factory-line batch labeling.

Cryptographic immutability and the audit ledger

Every consequential state change is written to an append-only AuditLog inside the same database transaction as the change itself. Each entry is SHA-256 hashed over its own fields plus the previous entry's hash, forming one global, insertion-ordered hash chain — the same structural principle blockchains use for tamper-evidence, without distributed-ledger overhead. Two independent layers of protection follow: a live integrity-verification function that mathematically proves whether any row has been altered, and a database-level append-only trigger that rejects any UPDATE/DELETE outright. A dedicated Audit Log interface lets an administrator filter, page, and export the full chronological trail, including a compliance-export workbook that stamps a live integrity-check result into the file at export time.

Role-based access and multi-party trust

A genuine multi-party trust model, not a single-tenant admin tool: manufacturers create and manage batch passports; independent auditors each hold their own individually issued cryptographic credential — not a shared role key, because a formal sign-off is only meaningful if attributable to one accountable person — and review submissions through a dedicated Auditor Portal; B2B buyers access their purchased-batch portfolio through a versioned Buyer API and read-only Customer Portal; upstream suppliers can be invited via a token-based, self-service submission flow without requiring full accounts; and consumers scanning the public QR code see only verified, publicly appropriate data — enforced server-side, not just in the UI.

ESG, compliance, and analytics layer

Computed fresh on every request rather than cached, so it can never disagree with the underlying records: PCR/PIR mass tracking and illustrative EU plastic-tax exposure; a certification compliance breakdown with live-computed expiry alerting; a transparent, weighted Sustainability Readiness Index; PPWR 2030/2040 recycled-content target tracking benchmarked against the regulation's published Article 7 thresholds; and a supplier performance/compliance ranking dashboard. Every computed score shows its full component breakdown and weighting rather than a bare number.

🛠️
Planned, not yet built — stated for transparency

Production-grade session authentication for the manufacturer/admin console (current interim mechanism uses shared per-role API keys, adequate for a pilot); a first-class batch-weight field so mass-based ESG metrics cover every batch, not only those where the carbon calculator was run; and formal third-party security/penetration review ahead of handling external paying-customer data at scale.

Before you file

Submission checklist & caveats

Four things to resolve before any of this becomes a binding application, carried over from the source package's own README.

1

Verify current program parameters before filing

Funding ceilings, co-financing rates, deadlines, and eligibility rules for NRW.BANK and EFRE.NRW programs change between calls. Re-check the live program pages immediately before submitting — the figures here were not locked in.

2

Financial projections are illustrative, not audited

Section 5's figures must be replaced with the founding team's own bottom-up model (headcount costs, actual pilot pricing, real pipeline) before being submitted as binding numbers.

3

No claims about certifications the platform doesn't hold

The platform supports ISCC PLUS / ISO 14021 / EN 15343 data capture and verification workflow — it is not itself a certification body. Never let grant text imply accreditation the product doesn't have.

4

Legal entity and eligibility details are placeholders

Company legal form, registered NRW address, headcount, and SME (KMU) status are the applicant's own facts to fill in — most programs restrict eligibility to a registered NRW establishment and defined SME size classes.
